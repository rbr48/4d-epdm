# Pre-registration

Written **before the candidate model exists**, and before any modelling choice has been
made. Recorded here so that the success criterion cannot be adjusted afterwards to
accommodate a disappointing result — which is how the predecessor project reached
"36/36 checks passed" while losing to a random walk at every horizon.

## Target

Annual real GDP growth (`NY.GDP.MKTP.KD.ZG`), 16 economies, forecast horizons h = 1..5.

Panel: JPN BGD KOR SGP CHN MYS THA VNM IND IDN PHL DEU USA GBR FRA NLD, 1990–2024,
560 country-years, 44 variables.

## Protocol

Rolling origin, 19 origins (2001–2019). At each origin the forecaster receives only rows
with `year <= origin`. Every parameter — including normalisation, feature scaling and
variable selection — is refit at every origin. Leakage is prevented structurally: the
future is simply not passed to the forecaster.

## The bar, measured before any model was built

Pooled RMSE across all horizons and countries:

| benchmark | pooled RMSE |
|---|---|
| **AR(1) + country FE** | **2.9286** |
| country historical mean | 2.9769 |
| random walk | 3.2531 |
| random walk with drift | 3.4980 |
| pooled panel mean | 3.5798 |

By horizon:

| h | ar1_fe | country_mean | pooled_mean | random_walk | rw_drift |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 2.739 | 2.847 | 3.589 | 2.966 | 3.024 |
| 2 | 2.918 | 2.954 | 3.592 | 3.341 | 3.486 |
| 3 | 2.956 | 2.988 | 3.572 | 3.319 | 3.556 |
| 4 | 2.994 | 3.031 | 3.575 | 3.267 | 3.584 |
| 5 | 3.026 | 3.059 | 3.572 | 3.357 | 3.794 |

Note the shape: AR(1)'s advantage is concentrated at h=1 (2.739 vs 2.847) and has nearly
evaporated by h=5 (3.026 vs 3.059). Any gain a candidate claims at long horizons must come
from somewhere other than persistence.

## Success criterion — PASS requires BOTH

1. **Lower pooled RMSE than every benchmark above**, i.e. beat 2.9286.
2. **Diebold–Mariano p < 0.05 against the toughest benchmark** at one or more horizons,
   with the candidate on the better side.

Loss differentials are averaged across countries before the test, so that 16 economies
falling together in 2009 and 2020 count as two shocks and not thirty-two independent
observations. Newey–West lag h−1 for forecast overlap, Harvey–Leybourne–Newbold
small-sample correction.

Implemented in `evaluation.py::verdict`, which is the arbiter.

## What does NOT count as success

- Winning at a single horizon while losing pooled.
- Winning on average without statistical significance.
- Beating only the weak benchmarks (pooled mean, random walk with drift).
- Winning on synthetic data generated by the candidate's own assumed process.
- Any post-hoc redefinition of the target, horizon set, sample, or benchmark list.

## Declared in advance: the likely outcome

Annual GDP growth is close to unforecastable at these horizons. The honest prior is that
beating AR(1)+FE by a significant margin is **unlikely** — perhaps 25–35%. The predecessor
model lost to a country mean by 13%, and lost by a further margin once the pandemic years
were excluded.

If the candidate fails this bar, the failure will be reported as the result, with the same
prominence any success would have received, and the project will be written up as a
negative finding about capability-style structural models rather than quietly rebuilt
until something passes.

## Secondary criterion (reported, not a substitute)

Because point-RMSE on annual growth may be irreducible, the harness also records interval
coverage and can be extended to CRPS and log score. A candidate that fails criterion 1 but
produces genuinely better-calibrated **densities** than benchmark densities is a real,
narrower result — and will be labelled as such, never as "the model works".
